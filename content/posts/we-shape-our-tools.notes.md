## Spine

Two levels, both anchored in the quote being a two-way loop:

- **Level 1 (back half, "our tools shape us"):** read as a warning (skills will rot), but that only counts losses. The quote is value-neutral: the tool makes us **different, not necessarily diminished**. Every tool in history subtracted a skill and we mostly took the trade. The real question is never what we lose (always something) but **who we become.** (Note: the crisp in-body statement of this was cut 2026-06-15; thesis now carried by the intro + close.)
- **Speed / adaptation (the new mini-spine):** the one new thing about AI is speed. Past reshaping was slow enough to keep up with; AI isn't. The real question isn't whether the tools change us (they always have) but whether they change us **faster than we can adapt.** The close pays this off (disruptive, but not all bad).
- **Level 2 (front half, "we shape our tools"):** agency. We treat the AI transition as weather, too big to push on. It isn't. The loop runs both ways (desire-path metaphor: enough walkers wear a line in the dirt and the path then steers the next walkers; emergent collective direction from many people over time). What feels cast in stone is mostly choices, individual and collective. We are not powerless facing the future.

ONE spine, not two (Joe, 2026-06-16): earlier drafts framed speed AND supervision as two separate novelties. Joe's call: it's really one thing, "can we adapt fast enough," and supervision is part of HOW we adapt, not a special second axis. So the supervisory point is now subordinate: the hard part of adapting is **judgment**, which we must both build (apprenticeship gone) and keep (the tool erodes the very judgment we use to catch it). "Supervision breaks the pattern" / "one reinvention is harder to wave away" CUT. The agency thread and the close are now one section (section 3, "We shape them too," merged 2026-06-16): aviation proves the erosion is adaptable, the realism beat keeps it honest (disruptive, can't promise we keep up), and it lands on agency, not disruption: "We are not powerless over how they shape us back." That landing IS the conclusion, per Joe's arc.

## Verified sources

- **Quote attribution** — Quote Investigator, https://quoteinvestigator.com/2016/06/26/shape/
  - "We shape our tools and thereafter our tools shape us": popularized by **John Culkin** in a 1967 _Saturday Review_ essay about McLuhan, presented as McLuhan's idea. No documentary evidence McLuhan coined it. Commonly misattributed to McLuhan.
  - **Churchill**, House of Commons, Oct 28 1943, on rebuilding the bombed chamber: "We shape our buildings, and afterwards our buildings shape us." The "buildings" template predates and seeds the "tools" version.
  - Thoreau precursor (_Walden_, 1854): "Men have become the tools of their tools." Not used.
- **Socrates on writing** — Plato, _Phaedrus_ (~370 BCE), the Theuth/Thamus myth. VERIFIED 2026-06-16: the linked Project Gutenberg page (1636-h, Jowett translation) is Plato's Phaedrus and contains the passage (Thamus tells Theuth writing "would only spoil men's memories"). Link is good. Optional: swap to Perseus for a more scholarly anchor; not required.
- **Aviation / automation dependency** — CHOSEN cite (option B, Joe 2026-06-16): Casner et al. 2014, "The Retention of Manual Flying Skills in the Automated Cockpit," _Human Factors_ 56(8): 1506-1516 (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0018720814535628). Finding: manual CONTROL holds up under automation; what degrades is the cognitive habit of staying engaged and ready to take over (depends on active supervising, not passive watching). The body was reworked to match ("the hands keep their touch; it's the mind that drifts"), which maps the example onto the post's judgment thesis. Footnote also nods to FAA automation-dependency work. (Alternative considered + verified but NOT used: DOT OIG 2016 audit, https://www.oig.dot.gov/library-item/32899, which supports the broader "manual skills degrade" claim; set aside because the Casner framing fits the thesis better.)
